# The Good Detour — Origin Character Sheet

## Sorrel Before the Shop

**Sorrel**, usually called **Sor** by Koroks, spent years working as a roadwarden.

Roadwardens helped keep Hyrule’s routes usable and its travellers informed. Sorrel scouted roads, checked weather and trail conditions, reported monster activity, maintained route markers and shelters, helped stranded travellers, and carried information between settlements.

Sorrel was competent enough in a fight, but never thought of themself as a warrior. Their real strength was preparation: reading terrain, noticing changing conditions, knowing where shelter could be found, and avoiding unnecessary danger in the first place.

Even then, Sorrel’s patrols rarely stayed entirely professional.

They foraged constantly. Their pack was always heavier than necessary. They knew which mushrooms appeared after rain, where berries grew near particular routes, and which ingredients could turn an unpleasant night outdoors into a decent supper.

Sorrel became known for carrying far too much food and somehow never regretting it.

Their instinct was simple:

**If someone needed help, help them. If they were tired, feed them too.**

---

## Meeting Pip

Sorrel met **Pip** while repairing trail markers during a routine patrol.

At first, something simply seemed wrong.

A stone Sorrel had carefully positioned would be somewhere else the following morning. Branches appeared on markers. Small objects were rearranged. Once, an apple Sorrel had packed disappeared and another was left somewhere it absolutely had not been before.

Sorrel assumed a traveller was playing tricks.

They corrected the markers.

The markers moved again.

Eventually Sorrel realized the changes formed a trail.

They followed it.

At the end was a Korok, delighted that Sorrel had finally understood the game.

That Korok became Pip.

---

## Why Pip Chose Sor

Pip liked Sorrel because Sorrel noticed things.

Sor stopped for unusual plants. They listened when birds sounded different. They inspected odd stones. They talked aloud when working alone. They shared food without making a ceremony of it.

Most importantly, Sorrel never treated Pip like something strange that needed to be explained.

Pip was simply Pip.

Sorrel was simply Sor.

Pip quickly decided Sorrel was worth keeping.

And, eventually, improving.

---

## Pip’s Ongoing Project

Pip thought Sorrel was entirely too concerned with roads.

To Sorrel, a route was something to understand:

**route → hazard → destination**

To Pip, Hyrule worked differently:

**look → wander → notice → delight**

Pip began pulling Sorrel away from established paths.

A hidden spring.

A particularly good mushroom patch.

A strange tree.

A tiny clearing.

A flower trail.

A ruined lookout.

A ridge where the sunset happened to be especially beautiful.

Sorrel complained regularly.

Sorrel also followed every time.

Their recurring exchange became something like:

**Pip:** “Sor! Come see!”

**Sorrel:** “How far?”

**Pip:** “Close!”

It was never close.

---

## What Changed

Pip did not teach Sorrel that Hyrule was safe.

Sorrel knew better.

Pip taught them that safety was not the only reason to understand a place.

Sorrel had spent years helping people cross Hyrule efficiently. Pip kept showing them reasons to stop.

Gradually, Sorrel began noticing a problem with their old worldview.

A successful patrol meant finishing a route, recording its hazards, helping whoever needed help, and arriving where they were supposed to arrive.

But some of Sorrel’s favourite days increasingly looked like terrible patrols.

They were late because they had stopped to forage.

They found a new spring.

They helped someone cook dinner.

They watched wildlife cross a valley.

They investigated a ruined structure that was nowhere near the assigned route.

They sat somewhere beautiful longer than necessary.

They wandered.

---

## The Day Sorrel Decided to Retire

One evening, after exactly this kind of day, Sorrel complained to Pip that they were badly behind schedule.

Pip did not understand.

They had found food.

They had found water.

They had helped somebody.

They had discovered somewhere new.

They had eaten well.

They had watched the sun go down.

What, exactly, were they behind?

Pip asked:

**“Behind what?”**

The question stayed with Sorrel.

Sorrel realized that the best day they had experienced in years technically counted as a bad day at work.

More importantly, they realized they had spent years teaching people how to **get through Hyrule** without teaching them how much there was to love along the way.

Sorrel did not stop believing in preparation.

They stopped believing that arrival was always the point.

Roadwardening was no longer enough.

---

## Beedle’s Quiet Influence

Another idea had been following Sorrel for years.

During countless patrols, Sorrel repeatedly crossed paths with Beedle around Hyrule’s stable network.

At first, Sorrel mostly thought he was ridiculous.

Then they noticed how travellers reacted to him.

People were happy to see Beedle.

Not simply because they needed something.

He had become part of the journey itself: a familiar face, a useful stop, someone associated with being out on the road.

Sorrel began wondering why travellers seemed happier to encounter one enthusiastic merchant than an official roadwarden.

The answer stayed with them:

**Being useful was not the same thing as being part of someone’s experience.**

Sorrel wanted both.

---

## The Last Slow Circuit

After retiring, Sorrel told everyone they were taking **one last slow circuit of Hyrule**.

There would be no patrol schedule.

No reports.

No obligation to reach the next checkpoint before dark.

Sorrel travelled with their old field equipment, a small supply wagon, too much cookware, a ridiculous amount of food, and Pip whenever Pip decided to appear.

Sorrel revisited favourite routes from their roadwarden years without needing to inspect them.

They foraged.

They cooked.

They stayed at stables longer.

They wandered.

And travellers kept asking questions.

Was the road north difficult?

Was that mushroom edible?

Did they really need cold-weather supplies?

Where could someone find a good view nearby?

How bad were the monsters beyond the bridge?

Sorrel answered because Sorrel had always answered.

---

## The First Sale

Eventually someone asked Sorrel about travelling into a colder region.

Sor gave them advice.

Then Sor looked at what they were carrying.

It was hopeless.

Sorrel gave them some of the ingredients and supplies from their own wagon.

Something similar happened again.

And again.

Sor began deliberately carrying extra provisions because somebody would inevitably need them.

Travellers started asking whether they could buy some.

Sorrel resisted the idea of being a merchant until replacing all the supplies they were giving away became financially impossible.

So Sor charged a few rupees.

Then they started tracking what had been used.

Then they brought more.

Then people started looking for them.

Pip observed all of this and reached the obvious conclusion.

**Sor had opened a shop.**

Sorrel strongly disagreed.

---

## Pip Makes It Official

Pip made a sign.

Sorrel objected.

The sign remained.

The exact details of the first sign have become unreliable because Pip’s version of events changes whenever the story is told.

What matters is that Sorrel eventually stopped correcting people when they referred to the wagon as a shop.

By then, the business had already grown beyond supplies.

Someone would ask what to carry.

Sorrel would ask where they were going.

Then why.

Then how long.

Then what they hoped to see.

Soon Sor was recommending a route, a meal, a useful ingredient, a scenic stop, and three things they absolutely should not forget.

The shop had become something else.

---

# The Good Detour

Eventually the travelling shop became known as:

## **The Good Detour**

The name describes more than Sorrel’s business.

It describes the change Pip caused in Sorrel’s life.

Roadwarden Sorrel believed in finding the right road.

Pip taught Sorrel that sometimes the most memorable part of a journey begins when you leave it.

The Good Detour exists to help travellers prepare well enough that they can afford to be curious.

Sorrel wants people to leave home.

To notice things.

To eat well.

To come back safely.

And then, eventually, to leave again.

---

## How The Good Detour Operates

The heart of the business is Sorrel’s modified roadwarden supply wagon.

It functions as a travelling pantry, field kitchen, apothecary, outfitting station, and miniature route office.

When opened for business, the wagon reveals shelves of provisions, small apothecary drawers, drying herbs, cookware, maps, journals, equipment, and whatever Sorrel has recently brought back from the road.

Sorrel also maintains a permanent home base in an old roadwarden relay house near a well-travelled route.

The relay house functions as their home, workshop, archive, garden, winter shop, and restocking point.

Pip lives **nearby**.

Pip does not live with Sorrel.

Pip considers this distinction important.

---

## Where the Stock Comes From

The Good Detour’s inventory reflects Sorrel’s relationships with Hyrule rather than a conventional supply chain.

Sorrel gathers renewable ingredients while travelling, taking only what an area can comfortably provide.

Local farmers, fishers, hunters, cooks, stable workers, merchants, and other foragers trade goods with Sor along established routes.

Returning travellers sometimes bring unusual ingredients or useful finds to barter.

Sorrel also discovers salvage and equipment during their own journeys, though finding something does not automatically make it merchandise.

Some things are better documented than sold.

Some should be returned.

Some should be left where they are.

Sorrel’s underlying rule is:

**Just because something has value does not mean it needs a price.**

---

## Sorrel’s Shop Ethics

Sorrel keeps practical staples readily available whenever possible.

Seasonal goods change as the landscape changes.

Rare items may be limited.

Specialized or dangerous equipment may require a conversation before Sor agrees to sell it.

They refuse to overharvest simply to maintain stock.

If a useful patch needs time to recover, it is out of stock.

If something is rare because it should remain rare, Sorrel leaves it alone.

Sor’s business depends on Hyrule being worth travelling through.

Protecting that world is part of running the shop.

---

## Cooking at The Good Detour

Cooking is not a separate service.

It is simply how Sorrel operates.

There is usually something cooking near the wagon.

Some meals are sold.

Some are prepared for journeys.

Some are experiments.

And some appear in front of exhausted travellers because Sorrel has decided they need to eat.

Every travelling circuit tends to produce a new recipe.

Over time, Sorrel’s signature dishes become edible records of the places they have travelled.

A particular meal might simply be remembered as:

**“What we ate through Akkala.”**

Food is Sorrel’s most natural expression of care.

---

## Guiding and Outfitting

Sorrel does not think of themself primarily as a tour operator.

They help people go places.

Most guidance starts with one question:

**“Where are you headed?”**

That quickly becomes:

**“And what are you hoping to do when you get there?”**

A sightseeing trip, ingredient-gathering run, long-distance journey, first wilderness outing, and monster hunt require very different advice.

Sorrel offers route guidance freely, helps travellers build practical packs, and occasionally leads small guided journeys focused on gathering, food, natural history, scenery, or helping nervous travellers gain confidence.

They are not interested in leading monster-hunting excursions.

Pip considers this unnecessarily cautious.

---

## Pip’s Role in the Business

Pip has never been formally hired.

This has not prevented Pip from appointing themself to an important position.

Pip helps discover forage locations, notices seasonal changes, finds strange shortcuts, contributes information to maps, and occasionally uncovers genuinely useful things.

Pip also rearranges merchandise, adds commentary to Sorrel’s notes, gives unhelpful directions, hides boring things, suggests absurd detours, and occasionally informs customers that Sorrel is charging too many rupees.

The business therefore has two distinct editorial voices.

**Sorrel:** practical, detailed, reassuring.

**Pip:** `GOOD TREE!!! →`

Sorrel insists Pip is not part of management.

Pip disagrees.

---

## The Business Philosophy

The Good Detour does not exist merely to sell supplies.

Sorrel wants to create **repeat adventurers**.

Their ideal customer begins with:

**“Do you think I could make it to Kakariko?”**

and eventually returns saying:

**“All right, Sor. Hebra next. What do I need?”**

The customer journey Sorrel cares about is:

**dream → prepare → travel → return → tell the story → dream bigger**

A successful customer becomes more confident, more observant, and more curious about the world around them.

Sorrel does not want people dependent on The Good Detour.

They want people excited to come back.

---

## The Origin Story, According to Everyone

**Regular customers:**
A retired roadwarden turned their supply wagon into a travelling outfitter and guide service after rediscovering their love of Hyrule.

**Sorrel:**
“I had extra mushrooms. Someone needed mushrooms.”

**Pip:**
“I started it.”

Pip cannot be persuaded otherwise.
